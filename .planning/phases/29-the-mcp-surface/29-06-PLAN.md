## Decisions and readings adopted by this plan

- **D-06** (locked): open/close per call with an explicit `store` argument on every
  verb. Two Phase 28 hazard classes become unreachable across calls by
  construction — `revertTo` returning a new handle, and a wedged
  `transactionStateUnknown` connection — because nothing is cached and nothing is
  reused. This deliberately reverses Phase 18's Rule A21, whose premise was
  avoiding a child-process respawn; D-02 deletes the child process.
- **D-07** (locked): every read verb takes a required image-path argument. An
  optional-argument-with-fallback hybrid was rejected outright, because an omitted
  argument would read as a plausible-looking success against whatever was recorded.
- **D-09** (locked): the disassembly-cursor verb is folded into the disassemble
  verb's address argument, not carried as a verb. Upstream's own procedure text
  says never to use the current cursor address.
- **Discretion 3, adopted with the reconciliation research already found.**
  Criterion 5's *"pre-validates every inner name and returns per-item status
  without aborting on the first failure"* and D-33's *"refused WHOLE"* are not in
  conflict: they are two phases of one call. The measured upstream note in
  `r2000-tools.ts:63-75` settles it — the per-call status reporting is orthogonal
  to our own refusal, which happens entirely on our side before anything executes.
  Two things the replacement adds that the original did not: an explicit **depth
  cap**, because the original recursion is unbounded and was safe only because a
  spawn cost dominated; and the inherited rule, verbatim in intent, that the batch
  verb is the ONE sanctioned exception to the nested-argument prohibition and no
  other meta-tool may join it.
- **C-2, adopted as research recommends.** `anno_save_project` is `curated` in the
  manifest, so a route is required, while the roadmap names an explicit save verb
  that governs durability an anti-feature. It is routed **honestly**: it opens,
  reads the current revision, closes, and returns that revision with a note stating
  that every write on this surface is already committed and durable when it
  returns and that this verb performs no write. Returning `{available:false, reason}`
  was rejected — a permanent refusal for a `curated` disposition is what the `omit`
  disposition is for, and the manifest does not say `omit`. The choice is recorded
  in the D-08 register by plan 29-08, because "a route that reports rather than
  writes" is exactly what a later reader would mistake for an oversight.
- **F-4, a Phase 29 obligation in writing.** 28-VERIFICATION.md states that the
  split-table re-interpretation disclosure must be **surfaced** when Phase 29 puts
  `set_data_type` on the MCP tool path, or the human never sees it. Both
  `contradictedComments` and `reinterpretedSplitTables` ride on a **successful**
  result and must reach the caller.
- **F-5, and why it earns a verb the manifest does not name.** `addScope` has no
  inverse, and this phase puts it on an agent-driven surface where a transposed
  span is likelier. One mistyped call makes every future scope from that start
  address permanently unaddable, recoverable only through the 32-revision ring.
  28-REVIEW's stated fix is to ship the inverse in the same phase as the refusal.
  `anno_remove_scope` appears in no manifest procedure, so it goes in the D-08
  register citing this finding as its named consumer — which is exactly what the
  register is for.
