<objective>
Build the reproducible-run protocol as one named procedure behind one optional
argument, and make it refuse rather than silently weaken.

Purpose: measured over `-binarymonitor` on this host, the sequence *connect (which
halts) → arm the frame anchor and the target while halted → `RESET` hard → exactly
one `EXIT` → wait event-driven for the target's own `CHECKPOINT_INFO` →
`REGISTERS_GET`* produced, at pre-protocol jitter of 0 / 1500 / 4000 ms, one
identical 64K sha256 and one identical `(PC=$ea31, hit_count=1, LIN=257,
CYC=57)`. The reset step is what makes that true; the protocol without it is the
control this phase records red.

`REPRO-02` says the protocol must be reached through an optional argument rather
than as a second route a caller can forget — so there is one procedure, one call
site, and no sub-flags. `D-14` makes it refuse when `frame_anchor` is absent: the
once-per-frame site is release-specific (a cracked release almost always takes
over the IRQ, so no KERNAL default such as `$EA31` is safe to guess), and refusing
with an error that names *why the frame term cannot be supplied* beats degrading
to the two-term oracle `REPRO-03` exists to guard against.

Output: `stock-reproducible-run.ts`, the two new argument keys with their manifest
declarations, and unit coverage of every part that does not need an emulator. The
live jitter triple and the reset-removed control are `33-11`'s transcripts.

it` section and its `AUTOSTART_SEQUENCE:` line, and implement that ordering. If it
recorded `AUTOSTART_FRAME_EXACT: not-achieved`, implement the READY-prompt
sequence measured green and record in the module header that the autostarted

## Assumption-delta architecture checkpoint (advisory, non-blocking) — FIRED

The detector returned `detected: true` with two signals, both landing on the same
sentence in `REPRO-02`. They are answered here, in the plan that implements the
transition.

**The `optional` signal — answered.** `vice_run_until`'s call shape is becoming
optional-argument-bearing (`reproducible`, `frame_anchor`), and the question the
family asks is whether the existing field is still the right anchor or whether the
anchor has moved.

- **The noun that is now primary: the *stop identity*** — the four-term record
  `(PC, hit_count, (LIN, CYC))`, owned by `src/mcp/vice/stop-oracle.ts` as
  `StopIdentity`.
- **Decision: `promote`.** The bare target `address` is demoted to **one term** of
  the stop identity (the `pc` term) rather than remaining the whole identity
  alongside it. Rationale: a stop certified by "PC reached this address" is
  frame-ambiguous — measured, two stops at the same instruction and the same
  anchor count differed in `LIN` by 21 and in 300 bytes of RAM — so the address
  alone was never the identity, only the most visible term of it. Promoting the
  record to primary is also what makes `CAP-03`'s structural bar meaningful: there
  is a named thing the captured 64K must not become a conjunct of.
- **Why not `add-alongside`.** Keeping `address` as a co-primary identity would be
  exactly the "second route a caller can forget" that `REPRO-02` forbids: a later
  capture could be stored as certified without ever passing through the four-term
  comparison, and nothing would flag it. That is the silent contradiction of the
  generalized intent the promote rule exists to prevent.
- **Companion invariant (accepted, wired into Task 1's criteria rather than a new
  task).** Every stop this procedure certifies round-trips through
  `compareStopIdentity` over all four terms — asserted by the answer carrying all
  four named fields and by `ORACLE_TERMS` being a four-member list the oracle
  iterates. A future phase reintroducing an address-only certification has to
  delete a field the test reads.

**The `pluralization` signal — detector false positive, recorded so it is not
re-litigated.** The hit landed on **negated** prose: `REPRO-02` says the protocol
is reached through an optional argument "**rather than** as a second route a
caller can forget". The requirement is *forbidding* a second route, not
introducing one. No `promote` decision is recorded on the strength of a negation.
</assumption_delta_decision>
