Both CAPTURE_EQUIVALENT values are the comparison tool's own result, not a narrative
judgement, and both comparisons are pasted verbatim in the capture records:

  danish  — two independent cold runs stopped at hit_count 1 and hit_count 2, i.e. in
            DIFFERENT game frames. 231 differing addresses in $0000-$CFFF, of which 100 lie
            outside every compare.mjs volatile region and differ by two or more bits
            (e.g. $00E9 $A7/$0C, the 45-address $CAE7-$CB71 sprite-shift block, the
            28-address $8183-$81FC sprite bitmaps). A comparison with 100 non-volatile
            multi-bit differences cannot return PASS.
  saeger  — both runs stopped at hit_count 1, i.e. in the SAME game frame. 41 one-bit
            drifts (12 in $0400-$CFFF, 29 in $E000-$FFFF) which pass, and exactly ONE
            non-volatile multi-bit difference: $00F6, $00 against $EB, the KERNAL's
            keyboard-decoding-table pointer, loader/KERNAL scratch sitting on the very
            boundary the instant is defined at. One divergence still fails the comparison.

The two results together isolate the cause: frame-index nondeterminism in the fork's
stopping checkpoint — it reports the hit but pauses ~a frame of work later, at a
wall-clock-determined instruction, and can straddle a frame boundary — is what produces a
structural divergence. Intra-frame position costs only one-bit drift. Neither is a fact
about the corpus.

## The aggregation rule, reproduced so a reader re-derives C0_CORPUS

From 23-03-PLAN.md Task 3, with the plan's `fail` mapped to SCHEMA.md's `partial` per the
resolution above:

  `pass`           if and only if CORPUS_RELEASES >= 1, AND every release has a 64-hex
                   CORPUS_FILE_SHA256 and a 64-hex CAPTURE_SHA256, AND every CAPTURE_SIZE
                   is 65536, AND every CAPTURE_EQUIVALENT is `yes`.
  `partial`        if any capture is void or short.   (the plan's `fail`)
  `could-not-run`  if the emulator could not be driven to a handoff at all.

Walking the recorded values through it:

  CORPUS_RELEASES = 2                              >= 1                        -> satisfied
  CORPUS_FILE_SHA256 64-hex for both releases                                  -> satisfied
  CAPTURE_SHA256 64-hex for both releases           could-not-run, both        -> NOT satisfied
  CAPTURE_SIZE = 65536 for both releases            could-not-run, both        -> NOT satisfied
  CAPTURE_EQUIVALENT = yes for both releases        no, both                    -> NOT satisfied

`pass` therefore fails on three of its five conjuncts. `could-not-run` does not apply: the
emulator WAS driven to the handoff, four times over two releases, and each instant was read
in a paused window and banked as a snapshot. Every capture is void — both runs of both
releases are voided in their own records under the template's rule — which is exactly the
`partial` branch.

## What that means downstream, stated so it is not mistaken for an accident

DECISION-RULE.md R1 is the first rule and fires on "`c0_corpus` is not `pass`", so R1
matches and the phase verdict is **no-go**. That is the pre-committed rule doing what it
was written to do, five plans before this measurement existed: without a capture proven
equivalent across two runs, the phase would be measuring another self-authored fixture,
which is the exact defect PROOF-01 exists to remove.

R1's own text names the consequence: the milestone becomes "secure a corpus first, or
re-scope v0.6.0 to a claim explicitly qualified as fixture-only". This file does not
narrow, refine or re-scope anything — DECISION-RULE.md is frozen and 23-10 owns the
findings document.

What IS established, and what a re-run would not have to redo:

  - Two named, hashed, independently-cracked releases of Bruce Lee (Datasoft, 1984) are on
    this host, identified by release id plus sha256, neither committed (D-04).
  - The loader/game handoff is `$1BC2`, the head of the game's own frame loop, identified by
    disassembling the running machine and re-verified per release on that release's own
    fresh machine. No fact used here came from dxa or Ghidra (D-06).
  - At that instant both releases show `$01 = $35` (BASIC and KERNAL banked out, RAM live
    under both), `$DD00 = $C1` -> vic_bank 2, `$D018 = $33` -> screen_base `$8C00`,
    charset_base `$8800`, identical sprite pointers, and PAL raster 311.
  - Four instants are banked as VICE snapshots outside the checkout
    (`danish_r1_handoff`, `danish_r2_handoff`, `saeger_r1_handoff`) and each was proven
    faithful on reload.
  - The two intros need DIFFERENT keypress routes: danish polls `$DC01` directly and needs
    `vice_keyboard_matrix` (fork-only); saeger spins on KERNAL `GETIN` and needs
    `vice_keyboard_petscii`. Each was established by disassembling the gate.
  - The single blocking defect is named and measured: the fork's stopping exec checkpoint is
    neither instruction-exact nor frame-exact.
