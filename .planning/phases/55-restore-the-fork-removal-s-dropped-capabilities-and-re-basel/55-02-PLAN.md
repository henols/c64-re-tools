<objective>
Give the incident record's `epoch_after` field a producer again, so a confirmed kill records whether the machine actually turned over.

MEASURED at `6e4e9a0b`: `grep -rn 'epoch_after' --include=*.ts src/mcp/vice/` outside test files hits `incident-record.ts` and nothing else. The sink accepts the field, defaults it to null, writes it into both the record's front matter and its prose body, and round-trips it on finalise. All three `finaliseIncidentRecord()` call sites in `stock-recycle.ts` pass only an outcome and a kill stage. So every incident record written since the fork removal records a null epoch.

The distinction lost is the one the record exists to preserve: "the machine was really replaced" versus "the kill was confirmed but the epoch never advanced" — the second meaning a recycle reported success without the instance turning over. The record is deliberately written BEFORE the destructive action so that distinction survives the kill; with the field always null, it does not survive at all.

The producer that was deleted also ran a readiness probe against the instance's HTTP endpoint. That module went with the fork backend and is not coming back. This plan restores the epoch poll only, which is the half that has a surviving mechanism — a dependency-injected epoch reader the handler already calls once, before the kill.

Purpose: restore a fact that recycle diagnostics depend on, in the module that owns recycle, covered by the suite that runs on every commit.
Output: a bounded post-kill epoch poll in `stock-recycle.ts`; both its branches pinned in `stock-recycle.test.ts`.
</objective>
