<objective>
Serialise every write-capable call through one owner (SESS-04), with contention
answered by a bounded FIFO queue rather than by a refusal, and prove it with a
lost-update planted violation that goes red when the lock is removed.

Purpose: this is **the go/no-go answer Phase 19 inherits.** Upstream's own
orchestration fans out to seven concurrent subagents against a single mutable,
non-transactional project file with no locking and no per-record versioning.
Deciding here, and proving it here, is what stops Phase 19 from copying that
model unchanged.

Output: a coarse mutex owned by `anno-session.ts` whose critical-section unit
is the whole `fn` a caller passes (so the mutating call and its own save are
already one unit, by construction); a bounded wait with a named busy error; the
D18-19 lost-update proof; and D18-16's reader-writer deferral recorded as a
decision with a Phase 19 measurement task rather than as an omission.
</objective>

SESS-04's clause "concurrent fan-out is restricted to read-only queries" is
satisfied at the **orchestration** level, not enforced mechanically at the seam.
The seam serialises everything, reads included; nothing in this phase prevents a
caller from fanning out mutating calls — they simply queue, and the observable
property SESS-04 names (parallel subagents cannot corrupt one session) holds
either way. The restriction that read-only fan-out is the *sanctioned* pattern
lives in Phase 19's playbooks. This is recorded here as an explicit assumption
rather than resolved, because "restricted to" is doing different work in the
requirement text than the mutex actually does.
