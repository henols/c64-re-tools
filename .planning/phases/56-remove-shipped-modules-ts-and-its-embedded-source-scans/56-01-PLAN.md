<cut_rule>
## What qualifies for removal

IN SCOPE: a case whose SUBJECT is the source TEXT of a module. The route does not matter. The
case may reach that text through the doomed scanning helpers. It may reach it through a raw
`readFileSync` of a sibling `.ts` or `.mts`. It may reach it through a module-scope `*_SOURCE`
constant built from one.

NOT IN SCOPE: a case that reads a non-source artifact. That means `package.json` parsed as JSON,
a JSON data file, a fixture, or a directory listing. This distinction is why `anno-seam.test.ts`
line 238 survives under D-07. The same test decides every other case in this phase.

- **D-01.** Whole-case removal is the default. If the case scans source text, the whole case
  goes. Not only the scanning lines.
- **D-02.** The one exemption. A case is exempt only when removal of every source-text assertion
  still leaves at least one assertion that exercises production behaviour. A remainder that is
  setup-only goes whole. A remainder whose assertions all derive from scanned text goes whole.
- **D-03.** An exempt case is stripped in place. Keep every surviving assertion line
  byte-for-byte. Never retype an assertion. Rename the case when the surviving name still
  promises something the stripped part proved.
- **D-04 (prohibition).** Never remove a case and re-add its behavioural half as a new case. That
  reaches D-03's end state by retyping. Retyping is how a case silently becomes weaker.
- **Helper rule.** A module-scope helper, constant or import goes with its cases only when every
  caller is itself being removed. grep the whole file for other callers first.
- **D-10 (prohibition).** Write NO new prose anywhere. No note. No seed. No successor document.
  No replacement guard. No lint rule. No weaker assertion. The phase SUMMARY is the one
  exception.
- **Stale comments in test files stay stale on purpose.** D-11's prose repair set holds five
  PRODUCTION modules and no test file. A test-file header paragraph that explains a now-removed
  structural check stays byte-identical. This follows D-13's reasoning.
- **Stop and report. Never adjust.** If a cut trips some OTHER file's own pinned assertion, that
  new measurement IS the finding. Report it and stop. Do not patch the tripped assertion.
</cut_rule>

<scanner_protocol>
## The D-14 scratch scanner. It narrows the read. It never authorises a cut

Read 56-RESEARCH.md § Q2 in full before you build it. Three binding constraints apply.

1. It must NOT import the doomed module's stripper. That module is being removed. Re-derive the
   same lexical rule inline instead. RESEARCH § Q2 names the rule. A `/` opens a regex literal
   only when the previous significant token is an expression-position keyword (`return`,
   `typeof`, `instanceof`, `in`, `of`, `new`, `delete`, `void`, `throw`, `case`, `do`, `else`,
   `yield`, `await`) or a punctuator other than `)`, `]`, `}`. Otherwise it is division.
2. It must separate comment and string text from real code. RESEARCH measured a naive stripper
   without the regex-versus-division rule. It found 14 of `vsf-slice.test.ts`'s real 36 cases.
3. Its output must be proved against a planted fixture before anyone trusts it. Observe the naive
   grep RED first. RESEARCH § Q2 carries the four-case fixture. GREEN means the scan flags
   exactly the two cases that genuinely consume a doomed symbol, and only those.

Write it to `/tmp/gsd-56-scope-scan/scope-scan.mjs`. It is NEVER committed. It is NEVER placed
under `scripts/`. D-14 forbids both. Do not list it in `files_modified`.

The scanner is symbol-anchored by construction. It has two measured blind spots. Both are handled
by hand.

- A case that reaches a doomed symbol through TWO levels of helper. Measured once, in
  `anno-seam.test.ts`.
- A case that scans source through a raw `readFileSync` with no doomed symbol in its chain.
  Measured in `stock-dispatch.test.ts`, `anno-export-asm.test.ts`, `anno-coverage.test.ts` and
  `anno-types.test.ts`.

So for every file, do three passes. Run the scanner. Then grep the file for `readFileSync` of a
sibling `.ts` or `.mts`, and for module-scope `*_SOURCE` constants. Then skim the file's own
surviving case NAMES for source-reading prose, such as "STRUCTURAL", "non-vacuity", "imports
nothing", or "declares no module-level". Resolve every candidate to its own paren-matched `test(`
boundary before you judge it. RESEARCH measured that line-proximity attribution overturns on 8 of
10 cases.
</scanner_protocol>
