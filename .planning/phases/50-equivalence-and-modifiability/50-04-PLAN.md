<objective>
Prove the live path end to end, once, on one binary, and calibrate the narrowed
mask against real run-to-run variation.

This is the phase's tracer slice. It wires one thin path through every layer the
phase touches: a committed fixture, a chosen load route, a real emulator, a named
logical checkpoint, a 64K capture, a chip-state sidecar, the new comparison
module, and a committed transcript. Nothing about it is a prototype. The
procedure it establishes is the procedure every later live task reuses without
change.

The slice is placed in wave 2 rather than wave 1 because ROADMAP criterion 1
requires the narrowed volatile mask committed before any rebuild is compared
under it. The instrument must exist before the slice that exercises it.

Purpose: to answer three questions that no later plan can proceed without. How
does a committed `.prg` reach a running emulator through the sanctioned tool
surface. What does one logical checkpoint resolve to. What do two runs of the
same binary actually differ by under the narrowed mask.

Output: a recorded load-route decision, two committed captures of the same
binary, and the transcript's capture-procedure and calibration sections.
</objective>
