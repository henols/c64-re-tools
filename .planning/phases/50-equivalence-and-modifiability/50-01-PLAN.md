<objective>
Build the instrument this phase measures with, and commit it before anything is
compared under it.

`compare.mjs` masks the whole of `$D000-$DFFF`. It also tolerates any one-bit
difference as drift. Both rules are correct for its own job, which is two
captures of the same binary at the same checkpoint. Both rules are fatal for
this phase's job. A regression that changes `lda #$02` to `lda #$03` ahead of
`sta $d020` is a one-bit change at a masked address. The existing rules would
therefore return PASS on a real regression, and would do so for two independent
reasons.

This plan writes a sibling module with its own rules. Those rules are a
narrowed I/O mask, no drift tolerance across two different binaries, an explicit
allowlist for intentional differences, and per-binary logical checkpoints. The
module lands in git before any capture exists to compare under it.

Purpose: ROADMAP criterion 1 requires the narrowed volatile mask **committed
before any rebuild is compared under it**. This plan is that commit. It is
deliberately placed ahead of the phase's tracer slice (plan 50-04). The phase's
own success criterion legislates that order. The tracer cannot lead when the
instrument it exercises must exist first.

Output: `compare-cross-binary.mjs`, its test file, and the SKILL.md text that
keeps the shipped skill honest about which module carries which rule.
</objective>

State the three rule departures from `compare.mjs` explicitly in the header,
each with its reason:
  (a) NO DRIFT BUCKET. Across two different binaries a one-bit difference is a
      real difference. `drift` exists to absorb sampling noise between two runs
      of the SAME binary; applied across binaries it would absorb an
      `lda #$02` to `lda #$03` regression whole. Every non-volatile,
      non-allowlisted difference is a divergence here regardless of bit count.
  (b) NARROWED I/O MASK. `compare.mjs` masks `$D000-$DFFF` entirely. This module
      masks only the addresses and register fields that genuinely cannot be
      stable, listed individually with a reason each.
  (c) ROUTE AWARENESS. A capture carries a declared `route` of `snapshot` or
      `memory-read`. On the snapshot route the bytes at `$D000-$DFFF` in the
      image are RAM under I/O, not the register read view (SKILL.md line 328);
      on the memory-read route they are the register read view. Comparing one
      against the other is meaningless, so the module refuses the pair.

Export and exercise two mask tables, kept separate and separately commented:

  `IMAGE_VOLATILE` — spans of the 64K image that are excluded from the verdict.
  Carry `[0x0000, 0x0001]` (CPU port), `[0x0100, 0x01ff]` (stack page) and
  `[0x0200, 0x03ff]` (KERNAL work area / BASIC input buffer) forward from
  `compare.mjs` with their existing reasons. Handle `$D000-$DFFF` by route: on
  the `snapshot` route those bytes are ordinary RAM and are NOT masked; on the
  `memory-read` route apply `IO_VOLATILE` below, folded over the VIC-II's own
  `$40`-byte register mirroring across `$D000-$D3FF` (mask offset `addr & 0x3f`
  within that window, so a regression at `$D020` is caught at `$D020` and at all
  fifteen of its mirrors).

  `IO_VOLATILE` — the register offsets and ranges that stay excluded, each with
  its own one-line hardware reason:
    `$D011` bit 7 is the raster counter's ninth bit, so the whole byte is masked
    `$D012` is the raster counter
    `$D019` is the interrupt latch
    `$D01E` and `$D01F` are the collision latches, cleared by reading them
    `$D400-$D7FF` is SID, write-only in hardware, read-back is unrecoverable
    `$D800-$DBFF` is colour RAM whose high nibble is open bus
    `$DC00-$DCFF` and `$DD00-$DDFF` are CIA, whose timers, time-of-day and
      interrupt-control registers all move without the program touching them
    `$DE00-$DFFF` is the I/O expansion area and reads open bus
  Everything else inside `$D000-$D3FF` is deliberately NOT masked. Name
  `$D000`-`$D010`, `$D013`-`$D018`, `$D01A`-`$D01D` and `$D020`-`$D02E` in a
  comment as the carve-out, and state that `$D015`, `$D018` and `$D020` are the
  three ROADMAP criterion 1 names inside it.

`compare.mjs`'s `compare()` does, producing `{ volatile, allowlisted,
