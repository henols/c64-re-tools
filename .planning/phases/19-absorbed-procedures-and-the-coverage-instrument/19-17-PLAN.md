Populate `PAYLOAD_TEMPLATES` with nine idioms, each emitted in the indexed forms named below plus one
immediate twin. Every prologue is written out as bytes with a per-instruction comment naming the
mnemonic and address, exactly as the committed payload constants are:

  1. `copy-loop` — two indexed loads then `rts`. No store, no jump, no push: nothing dispatches.

  2. `zp-vector-data-read` — two indexed loads consumed by `sta $fb` and `sta $fc`, then `ldy #$00`,
     `lda ($fb),y`, `rts`. The most ordinary 16-bit pointer idiom on a 6502.
     `provenLink: false`.
  3. `zp-vector-own-jump` — the same construction, then `jmp ($00fb)`: the jump names the vector the
     two paired loads built. `provenLink: true`.
  4. `zp-vector-foreign-jump` — the same construction plus two further stores into `$fd` and `$fe`,
     then `jmp ($00fd)`: a real indirect jump through a vector these two loads never wrote to.
     `provenLink: false`.
  5. `push-idiom-unlinked` — the same construction, then `pha`, `txa`, `pha`, `tya`, `rts`: two
     pushes and a return that carry bytes the two paired loads never supplied. This is the round-3
     blocker shape. `provenLink: false`.
  6. `push-idiom-linked` — a `pha` immediately after each of the two paired loads, each load's byte
     also stored to `$fb` and `$fc`, then `rts`: the two pushes carry exactly the two loaded bytes.
     `provenLink: true`.
  7. `push-idiom-out-of-window` — idiom 5 with one `nop` inserted before the `rts`, so the return
     falls outside the pairing window. `provenLink: false`.
  8. `zp-vector-nonadjacent` — two indexed loads consumed by `sta $fb` and `sta $fd`, two apart
     rather than adjacent, then `jmp ($00fb)`. Two stores that are not a vector.
     `provenLink: false`.
  9. `stack-return-canonical` — the class-4 five-instruction window: indexed load of the hi table,
     `pha`, indexed load of the lo table, `pha`, `rts`, with both loads through the same register.
     `provenLink: true`.
