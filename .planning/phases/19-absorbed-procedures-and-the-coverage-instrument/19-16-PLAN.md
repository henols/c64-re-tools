<objective>
Apply D-02's invariant to the SECOND true-returning site of `hasDispatchContext()`. D-03 is not a
statement about one branch: *every* true-returning site must consult the pairing under test. The
`zeropage-vector-jumped-through` branch was fixed in round 3 to require the CONSUMER — an indirect
jump naming the lower of two consecutive zero-page store targets — but it still collects those
store targets by scanning the whole window, with no link to the two loads actually being paired. A
payload can therefore build its pairing's vector at `$fb`/`$fc`, build a second, unrelated vector at
`$fd`/`$fe`, jump through the second, and be promoted on the strength of a link that has nothing to
do with the tables being reconstructed.

Purpose: this is the same defect class as the one plan 19-15 closed, in the branch round 3 believed
it had fixed — which is the evidence that round 4's scope is the class and not the instance (D-01).
Closing it here means both true-returning sites answer the same question about the same pairing, so a
future branch has one obvious shape to follow.

Output: `vectorLow` derived once in `resolveSplitOrientation()` and read by branch B; the foreign-jump
negative control and its one-operand-byte-different proven twin; the planted-violation record.

**Requirement scope.** `ABS-01`…`ABS-04` and `SURF-03` are closed by plans 19-01…19-05 and are out of
scope for this round. `COV-02` is cited because the dispatch gate is the mechanism both requirements
read. The open requirement is `COV-01`.
