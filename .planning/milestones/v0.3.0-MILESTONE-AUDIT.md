## What changed since round 1

+81 tests, every one of them a guard or a pin.

## The four things round 1 got wrong

This matters more than the closures, because round 1's errors were all the *same* error —
a scan that reported clean because it looked in too few places, which is precisely the
defect FLOW-02 was about. They are recorded in `corrections_to_round_1` and summarised
here:

| # | Round 1 said | Actually | Why it was wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| COR-01 | 8 undispositioned findings | **27** | A permissive heuristic counted any in-directory mention as a disposition; 20 sat in phases round 1 never opened, including **9 in Phase 08** with no disposition at all |
| COR-02 | WR-08 was covered | **Never fixed** | An 11-08 summary note about "WR-08 posture" applied only to that plan's new verbs |
| COR-03 | FLOW-02 had 2 sites | **3** | The grep was scoped to the two strings the integration checker handed over |
| COR-04 | 6 v0.2.0 stragglers open | **1 open, 5 already fixed** | Five were fixed in identifiable commits, never re-documented after a refix |

The guard-first principle earned its place by catching its own author.

## Why the guards matter more than the fixes

Four of round 1's findings existed *because a guard enumerated a list that stopped
growing*: `hostpath-consumers.test.ts` covered 10 of 14 r2000 modules, and
`check-skill-tool-coverage.mjs` validated `r2000_*` tool names in prose but never CLI
verbs. So every fix in this closure had to come with a self-deriving check, proven
non-vacuous by a planted violation rather than asserted.

Phase 11.1's verification mutation-tested all eleven guards against the live tree —
planting a real violation, observing the specific failure, reverting, confirming
byte-identity. Two results are worth keeping:

- Removing the IN-01 drain **reproduced real truncation** (146,176 of 512,000 bytes), so
  that finding was a live bug, not a theoretical one inherited from a review.
- The pre-fix WR-08 parser **actually created a file named `--entry`** on disk during the
  revert run.

A vacuous guard is worse than no guard: it certifies a false claim, which is FLOW-02's own
defect. That is why transcripts in plan summaries were re-derived rather than accepted.

Phase 10's security audit produced the most useful single fact in this closure: the
untrusted-input byte parsers (`r2000-d64.ts`, `r2000-project.ts`) are **not reachable
through any MCP tool**. They are imported only by `r2000-cli.ts` and a test-only gate —
never by `r2000-tools.ts`/`r2000-mcp-client.ts` — and the CLI dispatches at
`vice-proxy.ts:271` before any JSON-RPC object is constructed. A prompt-injected model
cannot feed a crafted `.d64` to them; it takes a human at a local shell. That is what
bounds WR-08's severity to a local footgun rather than an injection path, and it is worth
re-checking if anything ever wires those parsers behind a tool.

## Addendum, added at the v0.3.0 milestone close (2026-08-21)

*Appended by `/gsd-complete-milestone v0.3.0`. The verdict above is unchanged;
this records something the close found that the audit had not.*

**`docs-review-disposition.test.ts` — the `AUDIT-01` completeness guard this
audit credits plan 11.1-07 with building — was red at `4f048bb`**, the commit
whose subject reads `docs(v0.3.0): milestone audit round 2 -- passed, all
findings closed`. Verified by running the guard in a clean `git worktree` at that
exact commit, not inferred from the working tree. It reported three
undispositioned findings:

```
09-REVIEW.md (09-the-assumption-probe-go-no-go): IN-01
09-REVIEW.md (09-the-assumption-probe-go-no-go): IN-02
09-REVIEW.md (09-the-assumption-probe-go-no-go): IN-03
```

**Why both rounds missed them.** Round 1 enumerated Phase 10 and Phase 11 review
findings and never scanned Phase 9's review at all; plan 11.1-07's disposition
ledger was scoped by its own plan to Phase 10/11. The `tech_debt:` block above
carries exactly one entry, scoped `milestone-wide (after closure)`, which
`phaseScopedTechDebtText()` correctly declines to match against phase `09`. So no
disposition source existed for those three ids in any of the four places the
guard looks.

**Effect on the verdict: none, and that is a substantive judgement rather than a
convenience.** All three are `Info` severity against
`.planning/phases/09-the-assumption-probe-go-no-go/evidence/vice-tool-harness.mjs`
and `evidence/mcp-harness.mjs` — one-shot probe harnesses, absent from
`package.json` `files[]`, on no runtime path, and not part of any shipped
surface. `scores` and `gaps` above are unaffected.

**Disposition:** filed as
`.planning/todos/pending/2026-08-21-phase-09-review-in-01-in-03-never-dispositioned.md`,
which now carries the ledger row required by `docs-deferred-ledger.test.ts`. Its
recommended resolution is **`wont-fix` citing evidence immutability** rather than
an edit: the committed probe transcripts this milestone's `degrade`/`R4` verdict
rests on were produced by those exact files, and Phase 9's own evidence
convention is "literal `$` command + real stdout/stderr, never reconstructed".
Editing a harness after the fact would break the correspondence between the
script in the tree and the output it is the recorded provenance of — most
sharply for `IN-02`, whose fix changes argument-validation behaviour.

**The transferable finding, which is larger than the three `Info` items.** The
guard worked; nothing required anyone to read it. Four `docs-*.test.ts` guards
now exist (`docs-review-disposition`, `docs-deferred-ledger`,
`docs-dangling-refs`, `docs-linerefs`) and all four run under
`npm run test:automated`, but no step in `/gsd-audit-milestone`'s flow requires a
green run before an audit may record `status: passed`. Wiring that gate is the
recommended process change, and is the reason this addendum exists rather than a
quiet fix. Plan 11.1-07 built the right instrument and the audit was then written
without reading its last three lines.
